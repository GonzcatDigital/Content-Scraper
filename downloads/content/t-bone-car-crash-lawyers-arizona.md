## Understanding T-bone Car Crash  

### What Is a T-bone Accident? 

A T-bone car crash occurs when the front of one vehicle hits the side of another vehicle. It is also called a side-impact accident or broadside accident due to the location of the impact. Most of the time, a T-bone motor vehicle accident is an accident at an intersection, but it can also happen any time a car crosses a road or street.

The key in this type of car accident is that the cars are going perpendicular to each other. It is at this point that determining fault becomes critical. ### How Do T-bone Accidents Happen?

As with any car accident case, several factors can contribute to a T-bone car crash. While negligence is often at the heart of these accidents, various circumstances can play a role. Below, we outline some of the most common causes of T-bone accidents.

**Running a Stop Sign or Red Light:** 

One of the primary causes of a T-bone car crash is a driver running a stop sign or a red light. If another vehicle is already proceeding through the intersection with a green light or has the right of way, this can lead to a severe T-bone collision.

**Loss of Control of the Vehicle:** 

A T-bone accident can also occur if the at-fault driver loses control of their vehicle. When a driver experiences loss of traction or control, their vehicle might skid sideways into oncoming traffic, resulting in a T-bone car crash.

**Reckless or Distracted Driving:** 

T-bone accidents can be the result of reckless or distracted driving. Actions such as running a traffic light, making an improper left turn, ignoring stop signs, or driving under the influence all increase the likelihood of a T-bone car crash.

Understanding these common causes can help drivers stay vigilant and reduce their risk of being involved in Arizona traffic accidents.

## The Dangers of T-bone Collisions

### Why Are Side Impact Accidents So Dangerous?

A T-bone car crash is one of the most dangerous types of collisions on the road. According to data from the [National Highway Traffic Safety Administration](https://www.nhtsa.gov/), T-bone accidents account for 13% of all vehicle collisions nationwide. Alarmingly, these crashes are responsible for approximately 18% of all fatal car accidents. This highlights the critical need for increased awareness and safety measures to prevent such devastating incidents.

When a T-bone car crash occurs, it means that there is usually not enough vehicle to protect the passenger from the brunt of the force. Typically, the hit part of the vehicle collapses around the passenger compartment, failing to protect the person. Since a T-bone car crash occurs on the side of the car, there is simply not enough protection for the passenger. This results in T-bones being severe accidents.

## Determining Fault in T-bone Accidents

### Identifying the Responsible Parties

In a T-bone car crash, the car that did not have the right of way will always be at fault for the side impact accident. This is what Arizona Law says. However, the car that did have the right of way may also be at fault for failing to recognize what the other driver was doing. Unfortunately, there is rarely evidence at the scene to help determine which vehicle had the right of way. In this type of side impact accident, In most cases, authorities will determine who is at fault based on the statements of the drivers and witnesses. When both drivers claim they have the right of way, they will decide who seems more credible.

### Challenges in Proving Liability

In a T-bone car crash settlement, your car accident attorney will need to prove fault before determining your settlement amount. ## FAQs About T-bone Car Accidents

### Where Does T-bone Car Crash Most Often Occur?

T-bone car crashes often occur at intersections. The design of intersections creates the perfect environment for a side collision, especially when drivers are not paying attention.

When multiple vehicles pass each other at an intersection, there is a high risk of a T-bone car crash. Although intersections are designed to provide the safest possible environment for drivers, pedestrians, cyclists, and anyone else using them, they are considered the most dangerous places on the road.

However, side collisions can also occur anywhere on the road and are especially common at the meeting points between an alley and a lane and inside parking lots.

### What Happens to Your Body in a T-bone Accident?

The [severity of injuries](https://phxinjurylaw.com/blog/t-bone-car-crash-lawyers-arizona/) resulting from a T-bone car crash largely depends on the impact’s force and the overall circumstances of the accident. Generally, the greater the effect, the more serious the injuries are likely to be. In a minor T-bone collision, you might experience whiplash or a mild concussion. However, in a more severe accident, the consequences could be life-altering, such as a traumatic brain injury. Understanding the potential risks associated with T-bone car crashes is crucial for all drivers on the road.
