## What Not to Say After a Car Accident
After an accident, it is highly likely that the police will request statements from all drivers involved. It is crucial to understand what not to say during these statements. The most vital aspect to grasp is the importance of avoiding lies. Lies are difficult to sustain and can often result in more significant costs than telling the truth. It’s essential to remember that drivers are rarely the sole witnesses, and there may be objective forms of evidence, such as traffic cameras. Moreover, a false statement on a police report can severely harm a person’s credibility in court. Understanding what not to say after a car accident is paramount to ensure honesty and maintain one’s credibility in the legal process.

However, it is important to distinguish between lying and providing excessive information beyond what is currently known. For instance, some individuals tend to state that they are not injured immediately at the scene of an accident. However, it is crucial to understand what not to say after a car accident because serious injuries can often emerge days, weeks, or even months later. People present at the accident scene may sustain injuries without being aware of them. By being cautious about what not to say after a car accident, individuals can avoid making premature statements about their condition and ensure their well-being is properly assessed.

Likewise, individuals present at the accident scene often make statements admitting fault or offering apologies. However, it is crucial to understand what not to say after a car accident, as determining fault is a complex legal matter. Accidents are chaotic events with multiple potential causes, making it impossible for drivers involved to immediately ascertain their own culpability. It is important to exercise caution and refrain from making premature admissions of fault. By understanding what not to say after a car accident, individuals can protect their legal rights and avoid potential complications in determining liability.

## What Not to Say When Talking to an Insurance Company
These principles also apply when engaging in discussions with insurance companies, but there is another crucial aspect to consider. Insurance companies often aim to initiate settlement talks. It is essential for individuals to be aware of what not to say after a car accident when [dealing with insurance companies](https://phxinjurylaw.com/blog/5-sneaky-tactics-of-insurance-companies/). It’s important to understand that individuals have the authority to settle their legal claims at any given time. This means that an insurance company can contact someone who has been an accident victim and persuade them to agree to a settlement. Once a case has been settled in such a manner, it cannot be reopened. By understanding what not to say after a car accident, individuals can navigate conversations with insurance companies more effectively and make informed decisions regarding settlements.

Consequently, it is important for people who have an accident claim to [seek legal advice](https://phxinjurylaw.com/blog/choosing-right-personal-injury-lawyer/) as soon as possible. This is particularly true because insurance companies who see an accident victim with a strong claim will often call early in an attempt to settle for a low amount before the person has had the chance to talk to an attorney.

What not to say when talking to an insurance company: admission of fault, lies, discussion of injuries, settlement (unless you are sure that you are happy with that settlement).

## Insurance Companies’ Underhanded Tactics
After you’ve been in a car accident, the other insurance company’s representatives will likely be in touch with you. The best thing to do? Let them leave a message. Don’t return their calls.

There are four very serious reasons not to talk to insurance companies (other than your own, of course).

1.  **They seem friendly, but they’re not.** What insurance adjusters are trained to do is to get you to say something they can use against you in court. Their job is to save their company from having to pay you, even if their client is completely at fault for your accident and your injuries.
2.  **They’re trying to take advantage of you.** Unfortunately, many insurance adjusters will take advantage of the way you’re feeling right now. Most of us wouldn’t kick someone when they’re down, so we don’t expect others to do it to us; however, that’s what insurance adjusters are trying to do. They’re trying to get you to slip up when you’re at your most vulnerable.
3.  **They can use your own statements against you.** The worst part is that they’re recording everything you say. If your case goes to trial, their attorneys can use it against you in court. If you mistakenly said too much because you were confused, on painkillers, or for any other reason, you could irreversibly damage your case.
4.  **They may want to invade your privacy.** An insurance adjuster might ask you to sign a medical release, claiming that they want to see the records about your injury (for your “benefit,” of course). However, what this really means is that you’re granting them access to all of your past medical records, and they’ll comb through them meticulously to see if they can find a similar injury.
