# Arizona Punitive Damages: Wrongful Death Action

Did you lose a loved one in a car accident caused by someone else’s extreme negligence? In addition to dealing with the emotional and mental trauma of their loss, a family may face financial hardship.

If you have lost a loved one due to another party’s negligence, intentional wrongdoing, or failure to act, you may be entitled to punitive damages.

## Compensatory Damages vs. Punitive Damages
Wrongful death settlements are typically decided based on two types of damages: compensatory and punitive.

Compensatory damages include costs associated with any pain and suffering you experienced as a direct result of the accident, like:

-   **Medical expenses and hospital stays (past and future)**

-   **Recovery time or rehabilitation costs**

-   **Damage to your vehicle**

-   **Income lost to missing work**

-   **Emotional pain or stress as a result of the accident**

 Punitive damages, on the other hand, are given to the injured party and are meant to punish the at-fault party for the pain and suffering caused. This isn’t always black and white; juries may assign you partial fault (or negligence) for an accident or may find you were completely at fault, both of which could affect your ability to collect a settlement.

## Arizona Punitive Damages in a Wrongful Death Arizona Claim – Everything You Need To Know:
Arizona law allows surviving spouses, children, parents, and estate representatives to sue the party liable for their loved one’s death. This takes place if the deceased would have been able to sue the liable party had they survived the accident. This is known as a wrongful death action.

A wrongful death action allows particular individuals who were financially, or otherwise, dependent on the deceased to recover money for the damages they suffered as a result of their loved one’s death. While these damages can include compensation for a variety of losses, it is important to remember that the people being compensated in a wrongful death action are certain survivors of the deceased, not the deceased themselves.

As such, survivors will be compensated for their injuries, not for the injuries of their dependents. Depending on the specific facts of a case, the survivors may be entitled to compensatory, general, and Arizona punitive damages.

When a person wins a wrongful death claim, one of the things that can happen is they are awarded damages or money. Three types of damages can be awarded:

-   **Compensatory Damages.** These types of damages are also known as pecuniary damages and are generally used to compensate the victim for their injuries, treatment for their injuries, [lost wages](https://phxinjurylaw.com/blog/lost-wages-vs-lost-earning-capacity/), and property losses.
-   **General Damages.** These types of damages are often sought in conjunction with compensatory damages and generally are awarded when the victim has suffered a less tangible loss or an injury that is hard to specify. Such things as mental anguish, pain and suffering, and loss of companionship may fall under the category of general damages.
-   **Arizona Punitive Damages.** While the first two types of damages are meant to compensate the victim for their injuries and losses, Arizona punitive damages are meant to punish the wrongdoer and to deter wrongful action in the future by the wrongdoer and others. Arizona Punitive damages are also referred to as exemplary damages since they are supposed to set an example of what not to do.

Arizona Punitive damages are not awarded in all cases and may be harder to obtain than compensatory and general damages because the party seeking Arizona punitive damages must prove that the wrongdoer engaged in some sort of aggravating and outrageous behavior.

## Arizona Punitive Damages **in Wrongful Death Arizona Claims – Proof Of Bad Behavior:**

Under the common law, punitive damages are awarded in cases where the wrongdoer’s conduct was accompanied by an evil mind. This state of mind could be inferred by things such as:

-   An intent to harm someone
-   Interference with another person’s rights
-   Motivation to engage in wrongful conduct due to spite or ill-will
-   Consciously acting in a manner, or engaging in the behavior, that posed a substantial risk of harm to others

Arizona law recognizes that punitive damages may be awarded in common law cases where appropriate. Further, the Arizona legislature can grant a right to pursue punitive damages in the laws they pass, if they choose. 

In instances where a statute defines an offense, [Arizona punitive damages](https://phxinjurylaw.com/blog/punitive-damages-dui/) may be awarded if the law directly states that punitive damages may be awarded, or if the language of the law or statutory history indicates that the legislature intended that punitive damages are appropriate. This is important because under Arizona law the right to maintain a lawsuit for wrongful death is purely statutory, including the damages to which the wronged party is entitled.

## Arizona Punitive Damages **in a Wrongful Death Action**

In Arizona, wrongful death actions are governed primarily by three statutes, including Arizona Revised Statutes [12-611](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/12/00611.htm), [12-612](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/12/00612.htm), and [12-613](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/12/00613.htm). A.R.S. [12-613](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/12/00613.htm) details to what damages, or compensation, a successful party bringing a wrongful death action may be entitled.

While [Arizona Revised Statute 12-613](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/12/00613.htm), does not explicitly state that punitive damages may be pursued in a wrongful death action, Arizona courts recognize the language of the law as allowing an award of punitive damages if the jury sees fit. [A.R.S. 12-613](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/12/00613.htm) is broad and grants great discretion to the jury to “give such damages as it deems fair and just about the injury resulting from the death to the surviving parties who may be entitled to recover.” Additionally, the jury can consider “mitigating or aggravating circumstances attending the wrongful act, neglect or default.” ([A.R.S. 12-613](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/12/00613.htm).)

It is precisely the “mitigating or aggravating” language that Arizona courts have grasped to support punitive damage awards in wrongful death cases. This language was added to [A.R.S. 12-613](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/12/00613.htm) by the Arizona legislature in 1956 following the Arizona Supreme Court’s decision in [_Downs_](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1140537/downs-v-sulphur-springs-valley-electric-coop/)_,_ where the Court stated that plaintiffs in a wrongful death action were not allowed to pursue punitive damages. ([_Downs v. Sulphur Springs Valley Electric Coop_., 297 P.2d 339 (Ariz.1956.)](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1140537/downs-v-sulphur-springs-valley-electric-coop/)

Since the legislature added the “mitigating or aggravating” language months after _Downs,_ the courts have interpreted the legislative history as indicating that Arizona lawmakers intended that parties to a wrongful death action may seek punitive damages. (See, [_Boies v. Cole_, 90 Ariz. 198, 203, 407 P.2d 917 (Ariz. 1965](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1160638/boies-v-cole/?).)

Many states hold that since punitive damages are meant to punish the wrongdoer for their malicious behavior, if the wrongdoer is also dead then punitive damages cannot be awarded in a wrongful death Arizona case. This was the common rule for Arizona as well until 2001.

In [_Haralson_](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2514041/haralson-v-fisher-surveying-inc/?q=Haralson+v.+Fisher+Surveying%2C+&type=o&order_by=score+desc&stat_Precedential=on), the Arizona Supreme Court states that since punitive damages are not only meant to punish but also to serve as a deterrent for wrongful behavior from others in the community, there should be no outright prohibition of punitive damages being assessed against a wrongdoer’s estate. ([_Haralson v. Fisher Surveying, Inc_., 31 P3d 114, 201 Ariz. 1 (Ariz., 2001](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2514041/haralson-v-fisher-surveying-inc/?q=Haralson+v.+Fisher+Surveying%2C+&type=o&order_by=score+desc&stat_Precedential=on).) Thus, under Arizona law, a statutory beneficiary may seek punitive damages from the wrongdoer’s estate.

Like the common law award for punitive damages, which requires something more than intent or knowledge for an award, in Arizona, even if a law permits the pursuit of punitive damages the party seeking them must prove that the wrongdoer’s actions were the result of an evil mind while they engaged in aggravating and outrageous conduct.

## Evil Mind
In Arizona, if a law does not say the required mental state the wrongdoer must possess for punitive damages to be appropriate, then the courts defer to the evil mind standard. An evil mind can be shown by proving the wrongdoer had an ill will and intentionally harmed the victim, or participated in conduct that they knew caused a substantial risk of harming others.

In [_Rawling_](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1374504/rawlings-v-apodaca/?type=o&q=Rawlings%2C+151+Ariz.+at+162%2C+726+P.2d+at+578&type=o&order_by=score+desc&stat_Precedential=on)_s__,_ the Arizona Supreme Court states that a defendant acts with an evil mind if he “should be consciously aware of the evil of his actions, of the spitefulness of his motives or that his conduct is so outrageous, oppressive or intolerable in that it creates a substantial risk of tremendous harm _to_ others.” ([_Rawlings v. Apodaca_, 151 Ariz. 149, 162, (1986.)](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1374504/rawlings-v-apodaca/?type=o&q=Rawlings%2C+151+Ariz.+at+162%2C+726+P.2d+at+578&type=o&order_by=score+desc&stat_Precedential=on)

To determine whether a defendant acted with an evil mind, “a court examines factors such as:

-   The reprehensibility of the conduct
-   The severity of harm that was actually or potentially imposed and the defendant’s awareness of it
-   The duration of the misconduct, and
-   Any concealment of the risk of harm.” ([_Hudgins v. Southwest Airlines,_](https://casetext.com/case/hudgins-v-southwest-airlines) 212 P.3d 810 (Ariz. Ct. App., 2009.)

The wrongdoer’s state of mind may be proven by both circumstantial and indirect evidence and can be inferred from their conduct. When assessing whether punitive damages are appropriate and for how much, the jury’s focus should be on the wrongdoer’s state of mind, intent, and motive. Moreover, the focus must be on the wrongdoer’s conduct at the time of the incident, not the wrongdoer’s conduct before or after the incident. ([_Forquer v. Pinal County_, 526 P.2d 1064, 22 Ariz.App. 266 (Ariz. App., 1974.)](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2609662/forquer-v-pinal-county/) However, conduct after or before the incident, when viewed in its totality and with other evidence, may be used to prove an evil mind.

## Evidentiary Standard
For a jury to award punitive damages, the party seeking the damages must prove by clear and convincing evidence that the wrongdoer possessed an evil mind while they engaged in outrageous and aggravated conduct. ([_Hudgins v. Southwest Airlines,_](https://casetext.com/case/hudgins-v-southwest-airlines) 212 P.3d 810 (Ariz. Ct. App., 2009.)

Arizona Punitive damages may be more difficult to win because the party seeking them must prove to the jury more than that the wrongdoer was negligent. For example, negligence occurs when a person with a duty to prevent foreseeable harm to others fails in their duty, thereby harming others. A person can negligently cause a car accident because they were talking or texting on their phone while driving.

Although they may be liable for compensatory and general [damages](https://phxinjurylaw.com/blog/fatal-car-accident-settlement/) for their negligence, they may not be liable for punitive damages, since talking or texting on a cell phone does not, by itself, indicate they had an evil mind.

Examples of some things that have been allowed into evidence to prove that the wrongdoer acted particularly egregious and warranted an assessment of punitive damages include:

-   Failure of a motorist to stop and render assistance after an accident, taken in conjunction with other conduct and evidence. (See, [_Forquer v. Pinal County,_ 526 P.2d 1064, (Ariz. App., 1974](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2609662/forquer-v-pinal-county/).)
-   Statements made by the wrongdoer after the accident or occurrence that illustrate malice or hostility. (See, [_Forquer v. Pinal County,_ 526 P.2d 1064, (Ariz. App., 1974](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2609662/forquer-v-pinal-county/).)

For a jury to award punitive damages, it must find that the wrongdoer acted in such a way that a reasonable person would deem such behavior as outrageous, spiteful, malicious, fraudulent, or something of like sort.

## Limits on Arizona Punitive [Damage Awards](https://phxinjurylaw.com/blog/personal-injury-damage-awards/)
Although some state legislatures have enacted laws to limit the number of punitive damages that may be awarded in certain types of cases, the Arizona legislature has not. The [Arizona Constitution](https://www.azleg.gov/constitution/) states that “no law shall be enacted \[\] limiting the amounts of damages to be recovered for causing the death or injury of any person.” ([Arizona Constitution, Article 2 Section 31](https://www.azleg.gov/constitution/).)

While Arizona state law does not limit the number of damages a victim in a personal injury or [wrongful death Arizona](https://phxinjurylaw.com/blog/arizona-wrongful-death-statute/) case may be awarded, a good rule of thumb is that a punitive damage award should not be ten times greater than the combined compensatory and general damages, as this might be deemed excessive and unconstitutional. ([_State Farm Mut. Automobile Ins. Co. v. Campbell_, 538 U.S. 408 (U.S. 2003](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/127910/state-farm-mut-automobile-ins-co-v-campbell/?type=o&q=state+farm+v.+campbell&type=o&order_by=score+desc&stat_Precedential=on).)

## Who Can Receive Arizona Punitive Damages
Punitive damages can be awarded to the statutory beneficiaries, including surviving spouses, children, parents, or a representative of the estate. However, not all statutory beneficiaries may receive punitive damages. This is so even if they are awarded compensatory and general damages.

An award for damages is to be distributed to the statutory beneficiaries in proportion to the amount of harm they suffered as a result of the decedent’s death. Since each beneficiary may suffer differently, their award may be unique. For example, a minor child who was completely dependent on their deceased parent may receive a larger award than a sibling who is of majority age and supports themselves. Both siblings may be entitled to a share in the award resulting from the [wrongful death](https://phxinjurylaw.com/wrongful-death-claim-in-phoenix/) Arizona lawsuit, but the amount of money they receive can be different because they will have suffered different losses.
