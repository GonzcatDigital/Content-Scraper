E.K. sought the services of an [Arizona accident](https://phxinjurylaw.com/blog/arizona-car-accident-laws-leg-arm-injury-claim/) attorney when he became the victim of a careless driving automobile and bicycle accident. E.K. sustained injuries primarily to his back and wrist when his bicycle was struck by the at-fault driver. His medical bills totaled just over $20,000 and his ultimate settlement of $44,000 compensated him for his medical bills, pain and suffering, and bicycle damage sustained in the automobile and bicycle accident.

### The Scene
The automobile and bicycle accident occurred on January 19, 2016 at around 3:46 p.m. E.K., a 47-year-old male, was riding his bicycle eastbound on Guadalupe Road in the designated bike lane. The at-fault driver was driving his four-door sedan eastbound on Guadalupe Road in Tempe, Arizona, when he went to turn right into a gas station. As the at-fault driver started his turn into the gas station, he collided with E.K. who was riding in the bike lane to the right. As a result, E.K. went over the handlebars of his bicycle, hit the hood of the at-fault driver’s car and fell to the ground. Witnesses reported several teenagers getting out of the car after the automobile and bicycle accident and running off.

The [Tempe](https://phxinjurylaw.com/locations/tempe-car-accident-lawyer/) Police Department responded to the accident, conducted an investigation, and cited the driver who hit E.K. The Tempe Police interviewed several witnesses and received a statement from the at-fault driver that he did not see E.K. when he turned. But, he admitted that he did hit E.K. with his car.

### Applicable Law
The at-fault party was cited at the scene for violating [A.R.S. § 28-754A](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/28/00754.htm), improper turn. “A person shall not turn a vehicle at an intersection unless the vehicle is in proper position on the roadway as required in section 28-751, or turn a vehicle to enter a private road or driveway or otherwise turn a vehicle from a direct course or move right or left on a roadway unless and until the movement can be made with reasonable safety. ### Initial Injuries
The same day the automobile and bicycle accident occurred, E.K. presented to Urgent Care Extra where he was diagnosed with a contusion of the left forearm, left hand, right knee and left ankle plus abrasion of the left hand. He was administered Tetanus and Toradol shots and was applied cold packs to the affected regions. Thus, he was prescribed pain medications.

### Treatment and Medical Expenses
Just over a week after the accident occurred, E.K. presented to Eric. K. Cerre, NMD. Dr. Cerre diagnosed E.K. with a contusion of the left forearm, left hand and right knee, abrasion of the left forearm, right knee sprain/strain, lumbar sprain/strain and myofascitis. In order to control E.K.’s pain, Dr. Cerre administered two Lidocaine and Marcaine trigger point injections to E.K.’s posterior aspect of his left wrist and in his lower back. E.K. was treated a total of 3 times by Dr. Cerre over the course of two weeks.

Pursuant to Dr. Cerre’s recommendation, E.K. had bilateral x-rays done on both his right and left hand. The x-rays revealed no abnormalities.

E.K.’s total medical bills were $ 20,012.44.

### Additional Losses
At the time of the automobile and bicycle accident, E.K.’s bicycle was only a few months old. The total value of the bicycle was $2,787.16. E.K. was given $1,300 to settle his property damage claim. The property damage settlement was reached just over seven months after the date of the bicycle accident.

E.K. did not make an automobile and bicycle accident injury claim for [lost wages](https://phxinjurylaw.com/blog/lost-wages-vs-lost-earning-capacity/) as a result of this accident.

### Insurance Involvement
E.K. had health insurance at the time of the accident and used it to defray some of the upfront costs of medical care as a result of this accident.

Because this accident involved a bicycle rather than an automobile, E.K. did not have an applicable personal car insurance policy from which to draw additional compensation or an [auto accident injury](https://phxinjurylaw.com/blog/tips-on-handling-own-auto-accident-claim/) claim.

The at-fault driver had car insurance through American Access Casualty Company. Hence, American Access Casualty Company accepted full liability of this case.
