## 7 Steps for Your Medical Care Car Accident

### 1\. Pre-Hospital

At the scene of the incident, you will be dealing with first responders. Their job is to stabilize patients, assess the situation, and move them to the appropriate facility. These first few minutes and hours following the accident are critical for those who are injured. When traumatic injuries occur, patients are at risk of life-threatening complications, including shock as well as respiratory or heart failure. So knowing your rights for Medical Care Accident compensation is important.

### 2\. Acute Care

Medical treatment that occurs immediately and is short-term, for instance in an emergency room or hospital trauma center, is acute care. This is often the first step in the medical care car accident victims will receive.

Once the initial emergency is addressed and the patient is classified as critical or stable, they may be moved to a different section of the hospital. They may move to a specific floor that specializes in managing their pressing needs, including respiratory and heart issues or intensive care (ICU). In the emergency department, and following admission to the hospital, multiple services may be called for. These can include X-rays, medical scans, surgeries, and burn care.

### 3\. Trauma Care

Major, life-threatening issues are addressed by emergency-room specialists or a trauma team. Traumatic injuries often require immediate, and often complex, surgeries. In the US, trauma centers are classified into four categories, from [level I through level IV](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/trauma-levels). Level I is a comprehensive center that deals with serious car accidents and gunshot wounds, whereas level II centers provide limited care. This step is a very important part of the medical care car accident victims because it will help the victims recover from trauma caused by the accident.

### 4\. Sub-Acute Care

Following initial acute care, some patients require continued medical service before being discharged to return home. Skilled intervention, such as nursing care, various therapies, and educational programs may fall under the category of sub-acute care. It can be provided in special units of hospitals and outpatient facilities, long-term care facilities, skilled nursing facilities, or rehabilitation facilities. This is the common step in the medical care car accident victims will receive.

### 5\. Rehabilitation

Following a car accident, rehabilitation may be necessary for a short term or for a longer duration of time. Rehabilitative therapies address the physical, cognitive, and emotional issues that occur during and following a crash. Rehabilitation is the process of helping a patient to achieve the highest level of functioning, independence, and quality of life after an injury has occurred. It may help the injured person work to resume more normality as they move forward, an important part of the medical care car accident victims may need.

After a car accident has occurred, medical care car accident victims often are admitted to an acute care facility and then transferred to a sub-acute care facility. They may receive rehabilitation services at the sub-acute care facility and/or following discharge when they are back at home. The best outcomes occur when acute care and rehabilitation begin as soon as possible.

### 6\. Skilled Nursing Care

In some cases, medical care car accident victims require ongoing care in a facility setting where they can be attended to by a team of medical professionals. This type of care can be provided in a skilled nursing care facility, specialized hospital, or sub-acute facility. This may be covered by health insurance, but to qualify for the care and coverage, patients must require ongoing skilled care. It may be required for short-term or longer-term care. This step of the medical care car accident victim is vital for the recovery of the victim.

### 7\. Custodial Care

When victims suffer a permanent injury that is debilitating enough to require ongoing care for basic living needs, they may need custodial care. This can occur in a nursing home or treatment center. This differs from skilled nursing care in that custodial care does not need to be administered by medical teams. Instead, patients are cared for by licensed caregivers. Health insurance rarely covers this kind of care. This is also an essential part of the medical care car accident victims may need.

## Medical Guide: Medical Team

When individuals suffer traumatic injuries, they need a variety of specialists to care for them throughout their recovery. This medical team consists of surgeons, physicians, nurses, anesthesiologists, cardiologists, and neurologists, as well as many others as needs arise. The goal of the team is to work together to ensure the patient reaches optimal health and a strong quality of life. This professional specializes in helping medical care car accident victims.

### Physicians and Nurses

The attending physicians, surgeons, and nurses needed to support patients with traumatic injuries specialize in such scenarios. They are members of a trauma team or an emergency response team. They are experienced in making life-and-death decisions and help support victims in those first crucial hours and days following an incident.

### Anesthesiologists

A physician who has chosen to specialize in the dosage and administration of anesthesia is designated as an anesthesiologist. They support a trauma team during surgery to ensure the patient doesn’t experience any pain.

### Orthopedic Surgeons

Orthopedics refers to the bones of the body. When bones are broken in an accident, orthopedic surgeons are called in to consult or perform the surgeries. Some specialized orthopedic surgeons have expertise in certain areas of the body.

### Cardiologists

This surgeon specializes in matters related to the heart and cardiovascular system.

### Neurologists

Other specialty physicians and neurologists are experts in brain injuries. This includes not only the brain but the entire nervous system, extending to the spinal cord, nerves, and muscles. They will become part of your medical team when there are any neurological conditions or concerns.
